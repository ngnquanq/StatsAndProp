\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in high-stakes applications, yet their robustness against adversarial 
inputs remains insufficiently understood. This study presents a systematic benchmark of 20 adversarial test prompts across four
diverse categories: Math \& Statistics, Security \& IAM, Reasoning \& Jailbreak, and Hallucination, which are designed to evaluate
 the deception resistance of modern LLMs. We employ a hybrid evaluation pipeline using Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite as the target model
with Claude Opus 4.6 as an independent cross-model judge for API-evaluated prompts, producing quantitative robustness scores on a 0.0 - 1.0 scale.
 Our results reveal category-specific vulnerability patterns and provide actionable insights for improving LLM safety.
The complete benchmark framework, test suite, and evaluation pipeline are released as open-source tools for reproducible AI safety research.
\end{abstract}
