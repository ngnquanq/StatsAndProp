# Codex Round 1 — independent shortlist

Prepared independently, 2026-10-11; no Claude memo read. These are advisory candidates, not established novel results. Math scope: 6–12 months, fixed dimension or a fixed finite decision family. FE follows approximately one year later. Every pilot below is proposed, not executed. Planned results require an advisor to accept their mathematical substance; discovering an existing theorem is a reason to narrow or replace a topic.

## Three mathematics/statistics candidates

**M1. Partial identification of exploitation prevalence under incomplete reporting.** Target: prevalence of vulnerabilities ever exploited by a specified endpoint, within predeclared product/score strata; distinguish this from new exploitation in the next 30 days. Let Y denote exploitation and K catalog confirmation, with no false confirmations and reporting probability q in [a,b], a>0. Planned theorem: sharp bounds p(K)/b ≤ p(Y) ≤ min{1,p(K)/a}, simultaneous confidence regions, and robust comparisons between strata. Prove sharpness by constructing observationally equivalent latent laws; propagate binomial bounds under an explicit iid superpopulation model. The algebra is established identification logic; a useful contribution would be sensitivity-aware prioritization with reporting delays, not inventing PU learning. [Elkan–Noto](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~elkan/posonly.pdf), Lemma 1, is the constant-reporting benchmark; [FIRST methodology](https://www.first.org/epss/how-it-works.html) already describes PU learning. Public EPSS scores and CISA KEV support descriptive sensitivity analysis, not EPSS calibration against true 30-day labels: catalog addition dates can lag exploitation. Known-truth pilot: heterogeneous reporting and delay, measure interval coverage/width and ranking reversals. Fatal risk: unknown a makes bounds vacuous. Backup: inference for catalog-confirmation prevalence, explicitly changing the estimand.

**M2. Finite-sample auditing of access-policy reconstruction with clustered users.** Target: expected user-level mismatch between a mined policy and recorded entitlements, with separate omission and extra-assignment losses; this is an access-governance proxy, not permission legitimacy. Freeze a small family of attribute-based policies using training users. Planned theorem: simultaneous upper confidence bounds for bounded user losses under iid user clusters, permitting arbitrary dependence among permissions within a user; extend conservatively to an ε-contaminated user law. Proof: Hoeffding plus a finite-family union bound and a total-variation risk bound. Derive conditions under which selecting the simplest passing policy remains valid, and demonstrate failure of permission-row iid calibration. Established [risk-controlling prediction sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02703) and [CRC](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.02814v4), Theorem 1, already cover broad risks; [Unicorn](https://people.inf.ethz.ch/basin/pubs/ccs19.pdf) already mines policies from Amazon data. [UCI Amazon Access Samples](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/216/amazon) documents snapshot and change history, 11.7 MB, CC BY 4.0; it is not the Kaggle approval challenge. Known-truth pilot: latent policy, surplus grants, correlated permissions, rare roles. Outcomes: bound coverage, interval width, compression and review workload. Fatal risk: proxy validity or theorem contribution too weak. Backup: snapshot-only audit with transparent contamination sensitivity.

**M3. Authentication monitoring with an uncertain baseline and optional stopping.** Target: whether a prespecified conditional failure probability exceeds a baseline envelope, not whether a user is malicious. Planned theorem: for predictable q_t dominating the null conditional mean, products of 1+λ_t(X_t−q_t), with valid nonnegative factors, control crossing probability; add α+δ accounting when the baseline envelope fails with probability δ. Prove the supermartingale property by conditional expectation and apply Ville; compare this valid envelope with invalid plug-in estimates. [Howard et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.08240v7), Section 2 and Theorem 1, already supply time-uniform foundations. [LANL](https://csr.lanl.gov/data/cyber1/) supplies 58 days and red-team events, but failed logons appear only for users with some successful authentication; red-team labels do not certify all remaining activity as benign. Authentication download is 7.2 GB compressed: reserve disk and stream aggregates. Known-truth pilot: seasonal Bernoulli processes, dependence and a known change. Outcomes: false-alarm probability and detection-delay distributions. Fatal risk: no credible conditional baseline envelope. Backup: an iid synthetic theorem with descriptive LANL replay, subject to advisor approval.

## Three Financial Engineering candidates

**F1. Portfolio allocation stability under covariance error and turnover costs.** Target: out-of-sample variance and utility of a ten-sector long-only allocation, comparing equal weight, sample covariance and shrinkage. Planned theorem: a deterministic stability bound for the unique ridge-regularized simplex minimizer when covariance changes, using strong convexity; carry the perturbation into excess population variance. Concentration under fixed-p Gaussian iid returns is an analytic benchmark, not a theorem for actual returns. [Ledoit–Wolf](https://www.ledoit.net/research.htm) already established covariance shrinkage; do not claim a new estimator. [French industry portfolios](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/Data_Library/det_10_ind_port.html) are public constructed portfolios, not directly traded instruments; turnover-cost sensitivity is illustrative. Known-truth pilot: known covariance with regime switches and heavy tails. Outcomes: risk, weight instability and assumed-cost break-even points; block-bootstrap uncertainty needs dependence assumptions. Fatal risk: routine shrinkage backtest without decision insight. Backup: fixed-p covariance-error attribution study. WQU: Financial Data, Econometrics, Portfolio and Risk Management.

**F2. How yield-curve forecast uncertainty propagates to bond risk.** Target: one-month losses on prespecified hypothetical coupon-bond ladders, and adequacy of duration-only risk approximations. Use Nelson–Siegel factors with VAR/random-walk baselines. Planned theorem: simultaneous zero-yield error envelopes imply coupon-price brackets via discount monotonicity; derive a uniform Taylor remainder for duration/convexity approximations under bounded curve shocks. These are tractable analytic propositions, not new no-arbitrage pricing theory. [Diebold–Li](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/0404.pdf), Sections 2–3, already forecasts factors. [Federal Reserve GSW curves](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/nominal-yield-curve.htm) supply fitted zero curves, not raw trade prices; current vintages can be revised. Known-truth pilot: Gaussian factor dynamics and exact discounted cashflows. Outcomes: simultaneous yield/price coverage, interval width and risk-limit breaches. Forecast-band validity requires specified dependence assumptions; historical backtest coverage alone proves none. Fatal risk: merely reproducing yield RMSE tables. Backup: deterministic curve-error stress study with valuation bounds. WQU: Econometrics, Stochastic Modeling, Derivative Pricing, Risk Management.

**F3. Finite-budget option hedging under volatility misspecification.** Target: terminal hedging-loss distributions and cost/error tradeoffs for fixed-time versus delta-threshold rebalancing, at matched expected trading cost. Start with GBM, then one stochastic-volatility alternative. Planned proof: localized finite-horizon error bounds using Itô isometry and Lipschitz delta away from expiry; Black–Scholes prices are established benchmarks. [Fukasawa](https://arxiv.org/html/1204.0637v1), Introduction and Theorems 6/10, already gives optimal asymptotic cost/error theory, so “adaptive hedging is better” is not novelty. Public [SP500](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SP500) and [VIX](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS) can motivate parameter ranges, not identify an option surface or actual executable hedge P&L; use hypothetical contracts and explicitly assumed costs. Known-truth pilot: exact GBM transitions with known Greeks, grid-convergence and expiry sensitivity. Outcomes: tail losses, mean-square error, cost and Monte Carlo precision. Fatal risk: unavailable option histories or overly advanced stochastic calculus. Backup: daily GBM hedge discretization with a tightly bounded misspecification study. WQU: Derivative Pricing, Stochastic Modeling, Risk Management.

## Three sequential pairings

1. **M2 → F1:** recorded-access audit versus portfolio allocation; Amazon versus French returns; a confidence procedure versus a covariance/decision study. Transfer clustered validation, regularization and translating uncertainty into operational decisions.
2. **M1 → F2:** reporting identifiability versus interest-rate valuation; CVE/catalog data versus fitted Treasury curves; sharp latent-risk bounds versus bond-loss propagation. Transfer assumption audits, sensitivity analysis and interval interpretation, without reusing the theorem or empirical claim.
3. **M3 → F3:** monitoring probability versus hedge-loss/cost distribution; LANL events versus simulated asset paths and public market summaries; stopping-valid alarm accounting versus discretization analysis. Transfer filtration discipline and stochastic simulation, not a common martingale result submitted twice.

Keep separate datasets, primary theorems, repositories of results and manuscripts. Reuse only generic tooling with attribution. Provisional preference: M2→F1 for domain fit and workload, but M2's mathematical-substance gate is real. M1→F2 is the stronger sensitivity/identification alternative.

Local grounding: inspected multivariate lecture scope (fixed-p/iid), nonparametric regression derivations, renewal-process notes and one synthetic-data privacy review; the latter's novelty/AUC assertions were not adopted. Primary-source read depth above ranges from full data dictionaries and selected theorem/proof sections to Ledoit–Wolf's author summary; this is a targeted screen, not an exhaustive novelty audit. No personal files or experiments used. FE design remains conditional on WQU 690 Research Track approval/team rules; 692 would require a critical comparative review structure instead.
